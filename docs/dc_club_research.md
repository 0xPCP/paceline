## Per-Club Detail

### 1. Potomac Pedalers Touring Club (PPTC)
**Website:** potomacpedalers.org  
**Platform:** ClubExpress (pptc.clubexpress.com)  
**Location:** Washington DC / DMV  
**Members:** ~1,000+ active (979 on Strava club; described as "largest in the DC metro area and one of the largest in the US")  
**Annual dues:** $35 individual / $47 family / $25 student  

**Features:**
- ClubExpress ride calendar with month/week/day views, category filtering, capacity display
- Ride registration required ("helmets and registration required" for every ride)
- RideWithGPS club account — members download routes before each ride
- Photo gallery (member uploads)
- Insurance coverage on all sanctioned rides
- Ride leader program with safety training
- Vendor discounts (bike shops, travel, safety gear)
- Signature weekend events (centuries, multi-day trips)
- Facebook and Instagram social presence

**Gaps/Pain points:**
- ClubExpress UI is dated and aging; mobile experience lags
- No native Strava integration displayed on website (Strava club exists separately)
- No waiver signing visible on public site
- No club discussion forum/board visible
- No post-ride media sharing feature
- Calendar and registration are ClubExpress's but require member login for full access
- Newsletter not prominently featured

### Top 5 Highest-Priority Gaps for Paceline

1. **Route library / curated cue sheet browsing** — Almost every club uses RideWithGPS as a standalone paid service. If Paceline could replace this with an in-app route library (browse, preview, download GPX), it would eliminate a key reason clubs need two platforms.

2. **Newsletter / bulk email with archive** — 10+ clubs rely on regular newsletters to retain members. Paceline has news posts but no broadcast email with open tracking, segmentation, or archive. A newsletter module would make Paceline a true all-in-one.

3. **Pace group tagging** — Every serious road club (10+) labels rides by pace (A/B/C/D or mph range). Members filter and discover rides by pace group. Paceline needs a pace group field on rides, club-level pace group definitions, and filtering by pace on the Discover page.

4. **Strava leaderboard surfaced in club hub** — Clubs that have Strava integrations prominently display weekly/monthly leaderboards. Paceline's Strava sync could go further: surface a leaderboard widget inside the club, with filters by time period and segment.

5. **Member benefit/discount program** — Clubs use vendor discounts as a retention lever. Even a lightweight "member perks" section where admins can post discount codes/partner deals would differentiate Paceline.

| **Member directory** | Name, email, phone, address, membership tier, join date, renewal date. ClubExpress and Wild Apricot both export CSV. Joomla and WordPress custom installs require SQL export or CSV export. |
| **Membership dues history** | Payment records and transaction history. ClubExpress exports dues history. Wild Apricot can export payment records. Paceline would need a one-time dues history import or "grace period" for existing members. |
| **Signed waivers** | Digital waivers stored in Wild Apricot or SmartWaiver. Clubs will want proof that existing members already signed. Paceline would need a "waiver import" flag or bulk re-acceptance flow. |
| **Event/ride history** | Past ride records, attendance, and RSVP history. Not critical for migration but clubs value historical records. |
| **Photo albums** | Hosted on ClubExpress or Wild Apricot servers. No standard export — images must be downloaded and re-uploaded. |
| **Route library** | Typically maintained in RideWithGPS, not in the club management platform. Routes would need to be re-exported as GPX and re-organized in Paceline's route system. |
| **Newsletter archives** | HTML files or PDFs stored in ClubExpress/Wild Apricot. Clubs will want an archive link or PDF upload capability in Paceline. |
| **Forum posts** | ClubExpress has forum export tooling; Wild Apricot has limited export. Historical forum threads are rarely migrated; clubs usually accept a fresh start. |
| **Vendor discount codes / partner listings** | Typically just text/links; easy to recreate manually. |
| **Ride classifications / pace groups** | Club-specific definitions (e.g., "RBC Level 1 = 10–12 mph flat"). Need to be manually re-created in Paceline's pace group system. |

### Process Pain Points

| Pain Point | Details |
|------------|---------|
| **Member re-enrollment** | If Paceline cannot import existing dues status, every member may need to re-register. Clubs with 500–1,200 members will not tolerate forced re-enrollment — Paceline needs a migration-mode that imports paid status. |
| **Waiver re-acceptance** | Legally, clubs may need members to re-sign waivers on Paceline's system. This creates friction. Paceline should offer a "first login" waiver flow for migrated members. |
| **Admin learning curve** | Club admins are volunteers, often older. Wild Apricot and ClubExpress are familiar. Paceline's admin UX must be demonstrably simpler, not just different. |
| **RideWithGPS dependency** | Many clubs have invested years building RideWithGPS route libraries (PPTC, RBC, Princeton FW have 200–1,500+ routes). Clubs won't switch to Paceline unless they can retain their RideWithGPS relationship or Paceline can import/link to existing libraries. |
| **Email communication continuity** | Clubs send newsletters and announcements from their current platform. Migrating email lists and maintaining consistent communication during transition is critical. |
| **Calendar downtime** | Clubs cannot afford calendar gaps — their members expect weekly ride postings. Migration must be staged so the new calendar is populated *before* the old one is retired. |
| **Domain / URL continuity** | Clubs with established URLs (pptc.clubexpress.com, restonbikeclub.wildapricot.org) have links embedded in social media and member bookmarks. Paceline should offer custom domains or clean redirect paths. |
| **Board approval process** | Most clubs are 501(c)(3) nonprofits with boards. Platform changes require a board vote. Paceline sales cycles to established clubs will take 3–12 months. |

## Paceline Competitive Strengths (vs. These Clubs' Current Tools)

Paceline already provides features that most clubs are cobbling together from 2–5 separate services:

1. **Ride calendar + RSVP in one app** — No club has this natively; most use ClubExpress calendar + external sign-up or Wild Apricot + manual coordination.
2. **Post-ride media sharing** — Unique to Paceline; no competitor has this. Clubs email photos or post to Instagram.
3. **Weather-based auto-cancel** — Novel; clubs handle this manually via email/Facebook post.
4. **Strava integration with activity sync** — Paceline integrates natively; Wild Apricot and ClubExpress have none.
5. **GPX download** — Paceline has it; many platforms don't. But clubs want a full *library*, not just per-ride downloads.
6. **Waiver signing built in** — Wild Apricot has this but only 3 ClubExpress clubs use dedicated waiver tools.
7. **Private clubs / multi-admin** — Standard on Wild Apricot and ClubExpress but Paceline is competitive here.
8. **Ride Discover page** — Paceline's cross-club discovery is unique; no competitor offers cross-club ride discovery.
9. **Modern mobile-first UX** — Paceline's biggest differentiator over aging ClubExpress/Joomla sites.
